# How Kicksmash decides what to build

The daily checklist that goes with this yardstick is in `docs/OPERATING.md` (the service board, the weekly domain check). This is the yardstick for every idea, bug report and wish that reaches us, whoever brings it: a player on Telegram, a club by email, a developer in Discussions, or the proposal the app sends the owner the moment a note arrives. It is short on purpose. If a request fails a rule here, we say so kindly and honestly; if it passes, we ship it and tell the person what changed because of them.

## What we are

The first global padel match-up layer: one link that works in any chat, a card that lives in Telegram and Discord, three languages, an open API that people and their assistants use alike, and a level that belongs to the player. No app to install, no account to create, no staff, no paid tiers yet. We never name a competitor.

## The rules, in order

1. **One job per screen.** A screen does one thing. Anything extra lives behind one "More" line that summarises what is set. A request that adds a visible control to a screen already at its budget (one primary action, at most two secondary, at most seven controls above the fold on a phone) must move something else behind "More" or be declined.
2. **Defaults that need no decision.** Cost is optional, level is optional, listing is off, waitlist is on. New options ship with a default good enough that most people never open them.
3. **Discovery through use.** Groups appear after two matches with the same people; rankings after results; boards when a venue is typed. We do not offer a feature before it can be useful to that person.
4. **Words over widgets.** A sentence that explains beats a toggle that confuses. Copy ships in English, Russian and Spanish at once, with identical keys.
5. **The bots stay quiet.** One card per match, edited in place; new messages only for the card, "full", the reminder and the result. An edit is not a new message: the owner, 24 September 2026, "the stay quiet rule aims at preventing clutter and spam, which is the biggest pain point in WhatsApp padel groups", and an edit adds none. So the score nudge in a player's Telegram chat turns into the result card in place, and takes the court photo when one is added. A room that cannot be edited (a WhatsApp group) gets the card by hand, from the match page; nothing sends it there, and no player gets a card they did not already have a message for. No bumps, no nags, no explanations, nothing about Kicksmash beyond the card's footer. One private message sits beside these, by the owner's decision of 25 September 2026 ("the fourth spot offered to past partners and to players who want that time"): when a seat nobody is waiting for is free between 90 minutes and 48 hours before the match, the people who would take it (the crew, whoever asked for that hour, the players' partners of the last sixty days, the club's regulars when the match is on its board) hear once, in their own chat, with the card's one-tap join; once per match, never in a group. In a crew's own Telegram group (rule 31) a word ("in", "out") is answered with one reaction on the person's message, never a message; the notice is posted and pinned once, when the group opts in, and after a demotion the same notice is pinned again, never posted again; the admin who taps the crew link gets one reply when the link is too old, when they are not an admin of the group, or when the bot still needs the right to pin; and two things only the person can use go to them alone as an ephemeral message, or not at all: which of two open matches they mean, and why their word could not take the seat outright (a level to set, the organiser to accept), as the button's toast says it. "times?", which a person asks with its question mark, gets one reply with the free courts as buttons, at most one a minute in a chat, and the reply goes away when a tap makes the match (rule 36). After "Play again", each player of the last match gets one private message with the card's one-tap join, once per old match (rule 37, the owner, 11 October 2026: "Yes, one message each"). The organiser's answer to a join request and the move up from the waiting list reach a player who gave no working address in their own chat, once each (rule 33, the owner's decision of 16 October 2026, "Card and all channels"). One message a week in a crew's own group is the one exception to "only when asked". The owner decided it on 11 October 2026: "Once a week, before the weekly slot, the bot posts the best free times in a crew group that opted in, with no question asked. ... it is the one exception to the rule that the bot speaks in a group only when asked." Rule 36 ("The weekly best times") says when and what. A request that makes a bot talk more is declined unless the person explicitly asked to be talked to.
6. **Open and agent-native.** Everything public stays readable and CC BY 4.0 (free court times read from a booking platform excepted: rule 35); the API and MCP server are one key away for anyone; every crawler is welcome. A request that closes something is declined.
7. **Privacy by default.** First names only, no phone numbers or emails shown, public profiles off until switched on, nothing personal in Telegram cards beyond what the person typed there. Personal tokens and manage links never appear in public data.

   **The organiser sees whom Kicksmash cannot reach.** The owner decided on 15 October 2026. The question was: "May the organiser's match page name the past partners that Kicksmash cannot reach (no email, Telegram or push), so the organiser sends them the link?" The answer was "Name them (Recommended)": "Only the organiser sees it, only while a place is open, only partners from the last 60 days. Pro: you know whom to message (Tim, Adrian). Con: it shows that a named person gave Kicksmash no contact." On 15 October 2026, 13 of 16 past matches never filled, and the fourth place is where a match is lost. The free-seat offer reaches only a past partner that a channel reaches, 7 records of 21. For TDsv it reached Micky and Nicolai, and not Tim or Adrian, who both played with Erik. So the organiser's panel shows one line: "Kicksmash can't reach Tim and Adrian. Send them the link.", with the match's WhatsApp share. The names come from the offer's own list of past partners (`partnersQuery`, which `pastPartners` runs). A past partner played a match with the organiser, who has a seat in this match. That match ended in the last 60 days, and both people had a seat in it. An americano makes no partners, and a match that has not ended makes none yet. The names have no place in this match, are not its organiser, and did not just leave it. Each is the only record of its name among those partners. Each is also the only one of its first name, because the line shows first names. The match's level range admits them, and no channel of the offer reaches them on this deployment: Telegram, a WhatsApp number, an email with activity mail on, or push. A passkey is not a channel. So the line names the partners that the offer would tell and cannot reach (`admits` and `channelOf`, the offer's own two tests). It shows first names only, five at most, then "and 3 more". Only the organiser sees it (a manage link opens the organiser's view), and only on a match that has not started and has a place open. It is never in public data, the API, a card, a picture or a notice. A session wrote these details on 15 October 2026; the owner decided the line. A review of the same day found that the first version named the organiser's past players of any event, also an americano and a match still in play. The line now takes the offer's list. The line names only the organiser's own partners, because the owner's answer was "you know whom to message": a partner of another seated player is told by the offer but is not named, and an organiser without a seat sees no line (a session's choice of 15 October 2026, the narrower of the two). Since 16 October 2026 the line also says "they sign in as themselves" when that is true of every name on it: each name, typed on the plain link, signs in by "That's me" (rule 34, "A name on the organiser's unreached list"). Then the line reads "Kicksmash can't reach Tim and Adrian. Send them the link: they sign in as themselves." When "That's me" refuses one of them (an address, a passkey, a match of their own still to come), or the line has more names than it shows, the line keeps "Send them the link.", because that person still makes a second record. Since 16 October 2026 the privacy page tells players so, under "What we keep": an organiser can see the first name of a past partner whom Kicksmash cannot reach (an email counts only while its switch is on), and that Kicksmash cannot reach them (the owner's decision of 16 October 2026, "yes, write the privacy sentence"). `tests/legal.test.ts` checks that line, and its days from `PARTNER_WINDOW_MS`, in three languages. We decline a request to show these names to anybody but the organiser, or to show anybody's contact.
8. **Free tiers first.** No feature may require a paid plan (Vercel, Supabase, Resend, Telegram, Discord) below fifty emails a day. A request that needs one is "later", not "no".
9. **Small and finished.** A change ships with a unit test, passes typecheck, lint, the browser suites and a production check. Anything that needs a migration, touches sessions, authentication or personal data, or changes behaviour people rely on, is a design decision: it is a decision for the owner, never built from a note without their word.

   **A booking screenshot.** The owner decided on 11 October 2026. The question was: "When the key works, may Kicksmash read a booking screenshot with the AI model?" The answer was "Yes, landing page first (Recommended)": "The paste box gets "Add screenshot". Each screenshot makes one model call, and nothing is kept: not the image and not the reply. A separate cap allows 20 reads a day. ... The image can show names, and sometimes a phone number or a price. These go to Anthropic for the read (personal data, rule 9), at about 1 US cent for each image."

   So the paste box on the landing page is the first door for a screenshot. Kicksmash sends each image to Anthropic for one read. It keeps neither the image nor the reply. Twenty reads a day is the limit for all screenshots together.

   Only the image goes to Anthropic. The person's typed words do not go there: Kicksmash's own reader reads them. Kicksmash keeps no image, no reply and no log line of either. It counts the reads in `metrics_daily` (`paste_shot_ok`, `paste_shot_fail`, `paste_shot_off`).

   Kicksmash checks the key and the month's cost before each call, and then two limits. No call goes when Anthropic's last answer to our key was a refusal. No call goes when the month's cost reaches the owner's cap (`ANTHROPIC_MONTHLY_CAP_USD`). These two checks come first, so they take nothing from a person's limits. One address gets five reads a day, so one person cannot use all twenty. All addresses together get twenty reads a day. When a check stops the call, the box says in one line that it cannot read a screenshot now, and the text box still works (`src/lib/booking/screenshot.ts`). While Anthropic refuses the key, or while the month's cost is at the cap, the box does not show "Add screenshot" (`screenshotOffered`).

   Kicksmash does not trust the reply. It reads each field again with the rules of a typed paste, and a field in a wrong shape goes. The typed words win over the image, field by field. We decline a request to keep a screenshot or its reply, or to read a screenshot past these limits.

10. **Honest answers.** We tell people what we did, what we did not do, and why, in their language, in one message. We never promise a date. We say thank you when their note changed the product, and we name the change. We owe nobody an answer: the thank-you says the note was read and that the person hears if something gets built, never "within a day". A shipped message is one plain sentence in the first person, what changed and nothing else: no desk, no dates, no process.

## Verdicts

Since 11 September the owner decides what gets built. The moment a real note is acknowledged, the app sends the owner the verdict below together with what would change, a size, a timeline estimate and a recommendation (`src/lib/feedback/propose.ts`); nothing is built from a note without the owner's word, and the person hears only what shipped. The verdicts are the yardstick's answer, not an automatic action.


- **adopt**: passes the rules, fits in a day, has a test. Ship, verify in production, then tell the person exactly what changed and thank them.
- **later**: valid, but bigger than a day or blocked by rule 8 or 9. Record it as planned, tell the person it is on the list and why it waits. When it ships, tell them again.
- **decline**: fails a rule, or asks for something we deliberately do not do. Say which rule, kindly, in one or two sentences, and what they can do today instead.
- **ask**: unclear. One question back, nothing else. Never more than one.

## Voice

Warm, brief, European. First person singular is fine ("I read every note"). No hype, no sales language, no exclamation marks in a row. Names of other products never appear. Messages fit on a phone screen.

## Coaches

A coach's book is the first thing a coach uses that is not their own memory, so a coach's note weighs more than a cosmetic one. The rules above still apply; these add to them.

11. **Coach notes first.** A note whose author runs a book (the row carries `role: coach`) is read before the others and proposed to the owner first. A "how does this work" question is answered in the product's words, and if the answer was not obvious on the screen, the copy on that screen changes too. Nobody is ever told to talk to a person.
12. **A preference becomes a setting when three coaches ask.** Until then it is a default. Coach screens keep the budget: today's lessons and one action; a student sees the next lesson, the package line and one action; everything else behind "More". A request that adds a visible control for one coach's habit is "later" with that sentence.
13. **Their calendar, their sheet, their money.** We read and write the coach's own Google Calendar and package sheet where they share them; we never ask them to move. No payment passes through Kicksmash: the coach's PromptPay QR or payment link is shown, "paid" is a note the coach makes. A request for payments, commissions or a marketplace is declined with rule 6 and this one.
14. **Students are answered while the coach teaches.** Booking, cancelling, "how many lessons do I have left" and questions about the rules are answered at once, inside the coach's settings, in the student's language. The coach receives decisions only: a new student to accept, a time outside the hours. A request that makes the coach the bottleneck again is declined.
15. **Between lessons, nobody types.** A cancelled or blocked lesson comes back to the student as the nearest free times to tap; the freed slot goes to the first student waiting for it (then for that week), theirs for thirty minutes, then the next in line; a time outside the hours is one yes/no tap for the coach; the evening-before reminder and the "nearly out" note are sent once each. A request to make any of these a chat message, an approval or a setting is measured against rule 14 first.
16. **After the final point, everyone hears once.** The score nudge goes to every player on the channel they have (Telegram, WhatsApp, push, email), not only to the organizer: when the match ends (its start plus the length the organiser picked: 60, 90 or 120 minutes) and once more the next morning, only while no score exists. It read "two hours after the start" while every match lasted two hours; the lengths of 30 September 2026 made the end the moment, which is what the rule always meant. In Telegram the morning's replaces the evening's, and the answer turns it into the result card (rule 5). WhatsApp cannot edit a message, so there the nudge is the `ks_score_ask` template and the result card is one more message, the `ks_match_result` template, sent once per player per match and never again for a corrected score (the owner's decision of 26 September 2026, option 3.A). Any player may answer with a bare score ("6-4 6-3") in reply; the organizer's confirmation is a courtesy, not a gate: the first recorded result posts the card into the chat the match came from, with one line for the winners written per language and "same time next week?", which makes the crew a group with that match's own slot in one tap. The card with its share buttons waits on the match page; nothing asks a player to share it. A request for a third nudge, a leaderboard of who enters scores, or a share prompt after every match is declined.
17. **A reply is part of the note.** When a person answers our thank-you (Telegram reply, an email "Re:"), the text joins their note, dated, and the note goes back on the desk, even when it was shipped or declined. Nobody is asked to start over, and the three-message cap still holds: the "added to your note" line counts. In a crew's own Telegram group (rule 31), where the bot keeps none of the words, a reply does not join a note; /feedback there still makes one.
18. **Earned moments only, banter from the crew's own facts, and the photo is theirs.** A moment card exists for a first win, the tenth and fiftieth match, three wins in a row, ten different partners, a level that moved up a band, and a podium; each once per player, announced once, shareable by choice. Banter is the owner's decision of 25 September 2026 (option A): "facts only, from the players' own matches, shown only in the crew's own card and chat and on the result card picture, never on a public page". Two facts, one playful line each, never cruel, first names only. Three or more wins in a row, counting the match just scored, rides on the result: the group's result post, the result card in each player's chat, the card's page and its picture, so it travels when the picture is forwarded. A third or later late pull-out in 90 days (leaving within 24 hours of the start) rides on the match's card while the spot it opened is still open, as an edit, never a new message (rule 5). Neither line appears on a profile, a club or venue board, a ranking, `/built`, the API or the MCP, and no count of pull-outs is kept or shown anywhere. The organiser switches banter off for their matches, and on again, with one tap on the match page. The court photo behind a result is added by a participant, once, never asked for twice, and removed by whoever added it or the organizer; the result card already in each player's chat takes it by an edit. A request for streak counters on every card, badges for showing up, a reliability score, a public list of who pulls out, a ranking by worst days, or a share prompt after every match is declined with this rule.
19. **Coaches arrive on their own.** Every place a coach can be seen is a door to their own book: the front door at `/coaches/join`, the coach page, the club page, the city list, the foot of the player's directory at `/coaches`, the invite a coach forwards. Nobody onboards a coach by hand; the two introductions the owner makes are the exception that proves it. Each sign-up carries the door it came through, and the Sunday digest counts them, so a door that brings nobody gets redesigned, not marketed.
20. **The club watches.** A club's week is a template it fills once: socials, americanos, level nights. Every slot becomes a public match on the club's page a few days ahead, organised by the person who claimed the club; players sign up and pull out themselves, from the page or the card in their chat. The club never approves a sign-up and never types a roster; staff open the day view and see how the day will run. A request for the club to confirm each player, for a checkout, or for a notification per sign-up is declined with this rule.
21. **A level is a claim until someone who saw you play confirms it.** The organizer of a finished match, a coach at the club, or the club confirms a level in one tap; the tick holds while results keep the number within half a step. An event that takes confirmed levels only treats a declared level inside its range like an out-of-range one: it goes to the organizer's list. From there the player asks a coach at the club, or the club, in one tap; the confirmation seats them on every such list that now lets them in, without the organizer typing: their level fits the range, or the range does not hold them (below). A player who already sat in a finished, scored match with the organiser skips that organiser's level-range approval. A session added this on 11 October 2026, without a decision. The owner then decided the same day, after an audit: the question was "Three identity and level limits had no decision from you. Which of these changes do you approve?", and the owner chose "Keep the past-partner waiver". The reason a session gave: the organiser saw them play, so that organiser's range (below it, above it) lets them straight in. Only the range: a confirmed-levels-only match still takes a confirmed level, a missing level is still asked for, and another organiser's range still holds them (`waivedByPlay` and `admissionAt`). "Finished, scored" means both held a taken seat, taken at or before the start, in a match that started, was not called off and has a score, a match's sets or a scored round of an americano (`playedOut`): the score typed in at the court counts at once, because the "Are you in?" after "Play again" comes before the booked length ends. A seat taken after the start never counts, because joining stays open until the booked length ends and any seated player may type the score; and once a match has started only its organiser moves its start, so a latecomer cannot move the start past their own seat. A request for a test, a video review, a paid verification or a hierarchy of badges is declined with this rule.
22. **An Open repeats by itself.** The organizer of a finished tournament turns it into a series in one screen: a name and a rhythm (same weekday and time, every week, fortnight or month). The next edition exists before the page reloads; the rest make themselves a few days ahead, list themselves on the series page and the city page, and close themselves like any tournament. The organizer never types a date or a roster again, and can pause. A request for per-edition approval, a registration desk, entry fees through us, or a bracket is declined with this rule; five series per organizer.
23. **A pair is the unit of the serious tournament.** A competition has categories; a pair enters a category, once, and a player enters at most two categories in the same competition. A full category takes the pair on its waiting list in order of entry, and a withdrawal moves the first waiting pair up and tells them. A declared level outside a category's band cannot enter from the page; the organiser's desk can enter anyone. A request for a solo entry, a third category, or a queue that jumps is declined with this rule.
24. **A partner is a name until they claim the spot.** Whoever enters names their partner; the partner confirms by link, signed in or by typing their name, and the placeholder folds into their account. Nothing beyond a name is asked for the partner, and nothing is sent to a placeholder. A request to require the partner's account, phone or email at entry is declined with this rule.
25. **The fee is text and "paid" is the organiser's mark.** The competition shows the amount per pair and the ways to pay as the organiser wrote them; the organiser marks a pair paid. No payment passes through Kicksmash (rule 13 for coaches says the same). A request for a checkout, a deposit, or a refund flow is declined with this rule.
26. **We link to the stream, we never host it.** A big match gets a "Watch live" link the organiser pastes (YouTube, Twitch, Facebook Live); the page and the TV screen show it. A request for video hosting, a player, or a broadcast overlay is declined with this rule.
27. **The draw is made whole, published once, and a result is final once built on.** The organiser makes the draw from the field as it is (seeds and wildcards as they set them), looks at it, redraws or publishes; the players hear once where they start. Every phase is generated at once and fills itself in as results land. A score comes from the organiser or from a player of either pair and is checked against the rule of its round; it can be corrected until the next match built on it has a score. A request for a hand-edited bracket, a re-seed after publication, or a result entered by a spectator is declined with this rule.
28. **The schedule is one button, and the organiser moves what they must.** Every match of every drawn category gets a court and a time from the court list and the day's window: the groups first, a rest between a pair's matches, a round after the round it is built on, the finals last, never one player on two courts. The organiser moves one match with a court and a time; both pairs hear. Every player hears their own list once, and the court fifteen minutes before each match. A request to schedule by hand from a blank grid, or to remind more than once, is declined with this rule.
29. **A result is one reply, and the screen tells the truth every thirty seconds.** A player answers the match notice in the chat with the score and it lands on that match; the desk types it on the page; either way the tables, the brackets, the club's screen and the podium follow without a tap. The podium is a moment for every player on it, once (rule 18). A request for a live-score widget per point, a spectator's score, or a leaderboard of who enters results is declined with this rule.
30. **A group shows names, not levels, and may ask to join.** The owner's decision E, 9 October 2026: "What does a visitor see on a group page, and who can join?" — "names visible, levels hidden, optional Ask to join", for social proof and privacy. On the group page's member list, in `GET /api/v1/groups/{code}` and in the MCP tool `get_group`, a visitor sees a crew's first names and how many they are; a member's level there is for the group's own members. A match's own roster is not part of this: it still shows the levels of its seated players to anybody, by the match page's own rule, and every match on the group page links to its roster. The group's own range stays visible, because it describes the crew. An admin may switch on Ask to join (a ladies' crew, a level crew): a newcomer asks, with an optional short note; an admin says yes or no; a declined person may ask again after seven days. A seat in such a group's match is only a seat. Nor is a name a way in. The owner decided on 10 October 2026: "Need a shared match first". So "Are these yours?" folds a same-name record that is a member of such a group only after a shared match, and the automatic fold at the proof of an address does the same. The owner extended this on 11 October 2026. The question was: "Three identity and level limits had no decision from you. Which of these changes do you approve?" The owner chose "No admin role by name alone": "In a crew without "Ask to join", "Are these yours?" can move a crew's admin role by name alone. The change refuses that, as "That's me" already does." So a same-name record that created a crew, or is an admin of a crew, never folds by name. "That's me" refuses such a record with no exception, and this rule does the same. A shared match does not change it. A stranger of the same name can take a free seat beside the record in a match still to come. That makes a shared match. This is true for every crew, also for a crew that does not ask to join. Both doors ask it: the claim on the card and the automatic fold at the proof. A review read the owner's words in this way on 11 October 2026. The other reading lets such a record fold after a shared match that started and was not called off. The owner has not decided between the two readings. A shared match is a match that is not called off, where each record held a seat inside its capacity or was its organiser. A shared organiser, a shared club or a place on the waiting list is not a shared match (`mayClaim` in `src/lib/domain/sameName.ts`). A match still to come counts too, so a stranger of the same name can take a free seat beside the record and pass. The owner has not decided that case. For such a record, the card shows the days of its matches, but not the club and not the names of the other players, so that a stranger of the same name does not learn where to play to pass the check. For a record that runs a crew, one line in place of "Yes, these are mine" says so. A request to show members' levels to visitors on those three, to show who asked or what they wrote to anyone but the group's admins, or to make a match seat a way into an asking crew is declined with this rule.

## The crew's own chat

31. **The bot reads only where a crew asked it to, and keeps none of the words.** The owner, 9 October 2026: "players join a group managed by the Kicksmash bot so that it can assist setting up matches and do the admin, so there are no privacy concerns. The group with the bot that listens is solely for the purpose of coordinating padel matches." So the bot reads plain messages in one kind of place only: a Telegram group that an admin of that group tied to a crew through the link the crew page shows the crew's admins ("Run a Telegram group for this crew"), where the bot is an admin and its notice is pinned. The crew page asks for a new group only for the crew's padel. The link is signed for that crew admin and stops working when they stop being one. The first group that a crew ties stays the crew's group. The bot ties no second group to that crew, and another crew's link does not move the group. A session wrote this limit on 11 October 2026; the owner has not decided it. /quiet in the first group, or the bot's removal, frees the crew for another group. A first group that the bot is no longer in frees the crew too. The bot asks Telegram about that group once, when somebody taps the crew link in a new group. The bot stops reading when it loses the right to pin. The Bot API sends no update when somebody unpins a message. So an unpinned notice does not stop the reading, and a group admin types /quiet for that. Telegram's privacy mode stays on, so everywhere else the bot sees what it always saw: commands, and replies to its own messages (a reply "+1" or "can't make it" to a card takes or frees the seat in any group). In a crew's group it acts on four things and ignores the rest. A session wrote these fixed forms on 10 October 2026; the owner has not decided them. On 11 October 2026 a session added the common form with the question at the end (option C of the audit). Whether a model reads each line (option B) is the owner's decision. The first two things are "in" and "out" in English, Russian and Spanish, for the one open match. With two open matches, the bot asks that person alone, or says nothing. "me", "+" and their like count only as a reply to a card. A question, a forward or a channel's post never takes a seat. The third thing is "who's in Thursday 7pm Rawai?". It makes the match and posts its card as the reply, but only when the question names its hour outright. Its place must be a court the chat knows: its usual court, a court the sender played at, or a listed club named in full. Or it names no place, and the crew has a usual court. The question can also come after the match: "Let's play Thursday 7pm at Warehaus, who's in?". The words "padel" or "a game" name no place. A question with "no" and no court in it makes no match ("No padel tomorrow 7pm, anyone?"). A listed club named in full is its whole name, the name without "club" or "indoor", or every word that tells it apart. A city, a district or an everyday word such as "Asia" or "drama" never names a club. The owner, 10 October 2026: "Yes, but only on the club's own words". That match takes the day, the hour and the court, and nothing else from the chat line. A court the chat does not know yet goes through /new. A /new line in a crew's group puts the match on no club's board unless the line says "public". ## A phone is never shared: read this before any identity work

**A phone is never shared.** The owner decided on 28 October 2026. The owner's own words were: "assume phones are never shared. period". This is a standing design assumption. It holds for every change to sign-in, sessions, sign-out, passkeys, device hints, the notices about a new phone, and every other part of identity. Read it before you design such a change, and apply it before you ask the owner anything.

- One phone belongs to one person, and each browser on that phone belongs to the same person.
- We build no feature to protect a shared phone or a shared browser. We keep no feature for that reason alone.
- A feature that has another purpose stays, and that other purpose is its reason. "Sign out on this browser" stays: the owner decided it the same day, and it also undoes a wrong sign-in on a person's own phone.
- A question that exists only for a shared phone is not a question for the owner. Decide it from this assumption, and say so.
- Two phones of one model can look the same to a web page. That is not a shared phone: two people hold them. Rule 40 still guards against it.
- A forwarded personal link is not a shared phone either. The personal link still signs a browser in after "Sign out on this browser", and that stays as it is.

The assumption settled these on 28 October 2026:
- **The notice about a new phone keeps the gate of "Match changes".** The owner's question of the day was: "May the player's own name field offer a saved passkey above the keyboard ('Tim', then Face ID)?" The answer was "Yes, own name field (Recommended)". That change sends "A phone now signs you in" for each saved passkey. It asked one more question: "May a player switch the new-phone notice off?" Option A sends the notice on the own channel always, also with "Match changes" off. Option B keeps the gate of "Match changes", as for every notice. The question named two sessions that are not the player's: a shared phone and a forwarded link. The assumption settles the shared-phone half: no shared phone needs option A, so that half gives no reason for it. A session decided the forwarded-link half on 28 October 2026: the notice keeps the gate (option B, one gate, no new code). Rule 39 gives the reasons, and the owner can ask for option A. Rule 39, its ROADMAP.md entry, docs/OPERATING.md and the comment above `passkeySaved` in src/lib/domain/noticeKinds.ts say the same.
- **"Sign out on this browser" forgets this browser's key from the record it leaves.** The owner's question of the day was: "May a sign-in last one year after the last visit, and may every browser get 'Not you? Sign out'?" The answer was "Yes, both (Recommended)". The decision renews only full sign-ins, at most once in 30 days, and a sign-in by name still ends one year after it began. Under this assumption, the person who taps "Not you?" holds their own phone. So a key that was saved during that session belongs to the holder, not to the record. An example: Bob taps "That's me" on Tim's seat, on Bob's own phone, and Tim's record keeps the key of Bob's phone. Without the delete, the same browser later asks Bob "Welcome back, Tim?", and Tim's "Phones we recognise" lists Bob's phone. So the sign-out deletes that one key. The sign-out request sends the browser's values, as every door does (none in a European zone). The server makes the key and deletes that row of the record that signs out, in one statement (`forgetDeviceHints(db, id, key)`), and counts `hint_signout`. Its other phones stay. The server finds the row only by the key that the values make at the tap. A row that the same phone kept under other values stays: for example, the time zone, the languages or the text size changed after the save, or the phone is in a European zone now and sends no values. That row goes with "Forget this phone", or one year after its last use. The privacy page says so, and `tests/device-hint-doors.test.ts` proves the limit. This is the one exception to "The sign-out changes nothing else" (rule 34, "How long a sign-in lasts, and 'Not you?'").

What the assumption does not settle, and how a session settled it:
- A lasting "Not me" under "Welcome back, Tim?" (rule 40). It does not exist only for a shared phone: two phones of one model belong to two people. A session decided on 28 October 2026 (standing order 3) to keep today's "Not me", which deletes one key, until `hint_no` against `hint_asked` shows that the question comes back too often. The owner can ask for the lasting mark.

We decline these requests with this assumption: to build or keep a feature only to protect a shared phone or a shared browser; to ask the owner a question that exists only for a shared phone.

   **Part 1, the key.** The browser reads what it tells every site: the screen size, the pixel ratio, the languages, the time zone, the platform family, the touch points, the text size and the reduced-motion setting.    The browser now also sends these values. Each one is optional, and a missing one is "na":

   "Not me" deletes the key of this phone from that record. The question about that record can come back after the record's next join or sign-in from any phone that gives the same key, for example a second phone of the same model. A unit test proves this limit. A lasting "no" needs a mark that the next save keeps, and one tap of a stranger would then stop the question for the owner of the record in every browser of that key. A session decided on 28 October 2026 to keep the deletion of one key until `hint_no` against `hint_asked` shows that the question comes back too often; the owner can ask for the lasting mark. It is not a question about a shared phone ("A phone is never shared", above): the stranger holds another phone of the same model. "Don't recognise my phones" is the record's own lasting "no", and only the record's own full session sets it.

   The question never offers a record that another browser made less than seven days ago when that record shares no earlier match with anybody. An earlier match started before now, and nobody called it off. A match that is still to come is not an earlier match. The record and another person each have a taken seat in it, or the record organised it and another person has a taken seat. An organiser with no seat is not another person. So a newcomer who joins two posted matches today gets no question on the second match. Such a new record is most often the "second Tim": a record that somebody made in another browser, which still knows it. Two phones of one model give one key, so a question about such a record often goes to the wrong person. The browser that asks knows nobody, so every record in the scope came from another browser.

   One address may ask 20 questions in a day, as many as the taps of "That's me", and "Not me" and "It's me" count with them. One match takes 100 questions in a day from everybody, and "It's me" counts with them too. "It's me" takes these two rates before it reads anything, then it uses the rates of "That's me". A tap with no question costs a "That's me" attempt too. Counters: `hint_asked` (a question that the page shows), `hint_doors` (a question with doors), `hint_yes`, `hint_no`, `hint_ambiguous` (two records and more) and `hint_fresh` (the second Tim). The cost: one bounded read on an anonymous view of a match still to come. Outside Europe, nearly every such match has a remembered player, so the first anonymous view of it in a tab also makes one more call of about five statements (docs/OPERATING.md). `DEVICE_HINTS=save` stops the question and its call, and keeps the keys.
