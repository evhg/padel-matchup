# How Kicksmash decides what to build

The daily checklist that goes with this yardstick is in `docs/OPERATING.md` (the service board, the weekly domain check). This is the yardstick for every idea, bug report and wish that reaches us, whoever brings it: a player on Telegram, a club by email, a developer in Discussions, or the proposal the app sends the owner the moment a note arrives. It is short on purpose. If a request fails a rule here, we say so kindly and honestly; if it passes, we ship it and tell the person what changed because of them.

## What we are

The first global padel match-up layer: one link that works in any chat, a card that lives in Telegram and Discord, three languages, an open API that people and their assistants use alike, and a level that belongs to the player. No app to install, no account to create, no staff, no paid tiers yet. We never name a competitor.

## The rules, in order

1. **One job per screen.** A screen does one thing. Anything extra lives behind one "More" line that summarises what is set. A request that adds a visible control to a screen already at its budget (one primary action, at most two secondary, at most seven controls above the fold on a phone) must move something else behind "More" or be declined.
2. **Defaults that need no decision.** Cost is optional, level is optional, listing is off, waitlist is on. New options ship with a default good enough that most people never open them.
3. **Discovery through use.** Groups appear after two matches with the same people; rankings after results; boards when a venue is typed. We do not offer a feature before it can be useful to that person.
4. **Words over widgets.** A sentence that explains beats a toggle that confuses. Copy ships in English, Russian and Spanish at once, with identical keys.
5. **The bots stay quiet.** One card per match, edited in place; new messages only for the card, "full", the reminder and the result. An edit is not a new message: the owner, 24 September 2026, "the stay quiet rule aims at preventing clutter and spam, which is the biggest pain point in WhatsApp padel groups", and an edit adds none. So the score nudge in a player's Telegram chat turns into the result card in place, and takes the court photo when one is added. A room that cannot be edited (a WhatsApp group) gets the card by hand, from the match page; nothing sends it there, and no player gets a card they did not already have a message for. No bumps, no nags, no explanations, nothing about Kicksmash beyond the card's footer. One private message sits beside these, by the owner's decision of 25 September 2026 ("the fourth spot offered to past partners and to players who want that time"): when a seat nobody is waiting for is free between 90 minutes and 48 hours before the match, the people who would take it (the crew, whoever asked for that hour, the players' partners of the last sixty days, the club's regulars when the match is on its board) hear once, in their own chat, with the card's one-tap join; once per match, never in a group. In a crew's own Telegram group (rule 31) a word ("in", "out") is answered with one reaction on the person's message, never a message; the notice is posted and pinned once, when the group opts in, and after a demotion the same notice is pinned again, never posted again; the admin who taps the crew link gets one reply when the link is too old, when they are not an admin of the group, or when the bot still needs the right to pin; and two things only the person can use go to them alone as an ephemeral message, or not at all: which of two open matches they mean, and why their word could not take the seat outright (a level to set, the organiser to accept), as the button's toast says it. "times?", which a person asks with its question mark, gets one reply with the free courts as buttons, at most one a minute in a chat, and the reply goes away when a tap makes the match (rule 36). After "Play again", each player of the last match gets one private message with the card's one-tap join, once per old match (rule 37, the owner, 11 October 2026: "Yes, one message each"). A request that makes a bot talk more is declined unless the person explicitly asked to be talked to.
6. **Open and agent-native.** Everything public stays readable and CC BY 4.0 (free court times read from a booking platform excepted: rule 35); the API and MCP server are one key away for anyone; every crawler is welcome. A request that closes something is declined.
7. **Privacy by default.** First names only, no phone numbers or emails shown, public profiles off until switched on, nothing personal in Telegram cards beyond what the person typed there. Personal tokens and manage links never appear in public data.
8. **Free tiers first.** No feature may require a paid plan (Vercel, Supabase, Resend, Telegram, Discord) below fifty emails a day. A request that needs one is "later", not "no".
9. **Small and finished.** A change ships with a unit test, passes typecheck, lint, the browser suites and a production check. Anything that needs a migration, touches sessions, authentication or personal data, or changes behaviour people rely on, is a design decision: it is a decision for the owner, never built from a note without their word.

   **A booking screenshot.** The owner decided on 11 October 2026. The question was: "When the key works, may Kicksmash read a booking screenshot with the AI model?" The answer was "Yes, landing page first (Recommended)": "The paste box gets "Add screenshot". Each screenshot makes one model call, and nothing is kept: not the image and not the reply. A separate cap allows 20 reads a day. ... The image can show names, and sometimes a phone number or a price. These go to Anthropic for the read (personal data, rule 9), at about 1 US cent for each image."

   So the paste box on the landing page is the first door for a screenshot. Kicksmash sends each image to Anthropic for one read. It keeps neither the image nor the reply. Twenty reads a day is the limit for all screenshots together.

   Only the image goes to Anthropic. The person's typed words do not go there: Kicksmash's own reader reads them. Kicksmash keeps no image, no reply and no log line of either. It counts the reads in `metrics_daily` (`paste_shot_ok`, `paste_shot_fail`, `paste_shot_off`).

   Kicksmash checks the key and the month's cost before each call, and then two limits. No call goes when Anthropic's last answer to our key was a refusal. No call goes when the month's cost reaches the owner's cap (`ANTHROPIC_MONTHLY_CAP_USD`). These two checks come first, so they take nothing from a person's limits. One address gets five reads a day, so one person cannot use all twenty. All addresses together get twenty reads a day. When a check stops the call, the box says in one line that it cannot read a screenshot now, and the text box still works (`src/lib/booking/screenshot.ts`). While Anthropic refuses the key, or while the month's cost is at the cap, the box does not show "Add screenshot" (`screenshotOffered`).

   Kicksmash does not trust the reply. It reads each field again with the rules of a typed paste, and a field in a wrong shape goes. The typed words win over the image, field by field. We decline a request to keep a screenshot or its reply, or to read a screenshot past these limits.

10. **Honest answers.** We tell people what we did, what we did not do, and why, in their language, in one message. We never promise a date. We say thank you when their note changed the product, and we name the change. We owe nobody an answer: the thank-you says the note was read and that the person hears if something gets built, never "within a day". A shipped message is one plain sentence in the first person, what changed and nothing else: no desk, no dates, no process.

## Verdicts

Since 11 September the owner decides what gets built. The moment a real note is acknowledged, the app sends the owner the verdict below together with what would change, a size, a timeline estimate and a recommendation (`src/lib/feedback/propose.ts`); nothing is built from a note without the owner's word, and the person hears only what shipped. The verdicts are the yardstick's answer, not an automatic action.


- **adopt**: passes the rules, fits in a day, has a test. Ship, verify in production, then tell the person exactly what changed and thank them.
- **later**: valid, but bigger than a day or blocked by rule 8 or 9. Record it as planned, tell the person it is on the list and why it waits. When it ships, tell them again.
- **decline**: fails a rule, or asks for something we deliberately do not do. Say which rule, kindly, in one or two sentences, and what they can do today instead.
- **ask**: unclear. One question back, nothing else. Never more than one.

## Voice

Warm, brief, European. First person singular is fine ("I read every note"). No hype, no sales language, no exclamation marks in a row. Names of other products never appear. Messages fit on a phone screen.

## Coaches

A coach's book is the first thing a coach uses that is not their own memory, so a coach's note weighs more than a cosmetic one. The rules above still apply; these add to them.

11. **Coach notes first.** A note whose author runs a book (the row carries `role: coach`) is read before the others and proposed to the owner first. A "how does this work" question is answered in the product's words, and if the answer was not obvious on the screen, the copy on that screen changes too. Nobody is ever told to talk to a person.
12. **A preference becomes a setting when three coaches ask.** Until then it is a default. Coach screens keep the budget: today's lessons and one action; a student sees the next lesson, the package line and one action; everything else behind "More". A request that adds a visible control for one coach's habit is "later" with that sentence.
13. **Their calendar, their sheet, their money.** We read and write the coach's own Google Calendar and package sheet where they share them; we never ask them to move. No payment passes through Kicksmash: the coach's PromptPay QR or payment link is shown, "paid" is a note the coach makes. A request for payments, commissions or a marketplace is declined with rule 6 and this one.
14. **Students are answered while the coach teaches.** Booking, cancelling, "how many lessons do I have left" and questions about the rules are answered at once, inside the coach's settings, in the student's language. The coach receives decisions only: a new student to accept, a time outside the hours. A request that makes the coach the bottleneck again is declined.
15. **Between lessons, nobody types.** A cancelled or blocked lesson comes back to the student as the nearest free times to tap; the freed slot goes to the first student waiting for it (then for that week), theirs for thirty minutes, then the next in line; a time outside the hours is one yes/no tap for the coach; the evening-before reminder and the "nearly out" note are sent once each. A request to make any of these a chat message, an approval or a setting is measured against rule 14 first.
16. **After the final point, everyone hears once.** The score nudge goes to every player on the channel they have (Telegram, WhatsApp, push, email), not only to the organizer: when the match ends (its start plus the length the organiser picked: 60, 90 or 120 minutes) and once more the next morning, only while no score exists. It read "two hours after the start" while every match lasted two hours; the lengths of 30 September 2026 made the end the moment, which is what the rule always meant. In Telegram the morning's replaces the evening's, and the answer turns it into the result card (rule 5). WhatsApp cannot edit a message, so there the nudge is the `ks_score_ask` template and the result card is one more message, the `ks_match_result` template, sent once per player per match and never again for a corrected score (the owner's decision of 26 September 2026, option 3.A). Any player may answer with a bare score ("6-4 6-3") in reply; the organizer's confirmation is a courtesy, not a gate: the first recorded result posts the card into the chat the match came from, with one line for the winners written per language and "same time next week?", which makes the crew a group with that match's own slot in one tap. The card with its share buttons waits on the match page; nothing asks a player to share it. A request for a third nudge, a leaderboard of who enters scores, or a share prompt after every match is declined.
17. **A reply is part of the note.** When a person answers our thank-you (Telegram reply, an email "Re:"), the text joins their note, dated, and the note goes back on the desk, even when it was shipped or declined. Nobody is asked to start over, and the three-message cap still holds: the "added to your note" line counts. In a crew's own Telegram group (rule 31), where the bot keeps none of the words, a reply does not join a note; /feedback there still makes one.
18. **Earned moments only, banter from the crew's own facts, and the photo is theirs.** A moment card exists for a first win, the tenth and fiftieth match, three wins in a row, ten different partners, a level that moved up a band, and a podium; each once per player, announced once, shareable by choice. Banter is the owner's decision of 25 September 2026 (option A): "facts only, from the players' own matches, shown only in the crew's own card and chat and on the result card picture, never on a public page". Two facts, one playful line each, never cruel, first names only. Three or more wins in a row, counting the match just scored, rides on the result: the group's result post, the result card in each player's chat, the card's page and its picture, so it travels when the picture is forwarded. A third or later late pull-out in 90 days (leaving within 24 hours of the start) rides on the match's card while the spot it opened is still open, as an edit, never a new message (rule 5). Neither line appears on a profile, a club or venue board, a ranking, `/built`, the API or the MCP, and no count of pull-outs is kept or shown anywhere. The organiser switches banter off for their matches, and on again, with one tap on the match page. The court photo behind a result is added by a participant, once, never asked for twice, and removed by whoever added it or the organizer; the result card already in each player's chat takes it by an edit. A request for streak counters on every card, badges for showing up, a reliability score, a public list of who pulls out, a ranking by worst days, or a share prompt after every match is declined with this rule.
19. **Coaches arrive on their own.** Every place a coach can be seen is a door to their own book: the front door at `/coaches/join`, the coach page, the club page, the city list, the foot of the player's directory at `/coaches`, the invite a coach forwards. Nobody onboards a coach by hand; the two introductions the owner makes are the exception that proves it. Each sign-up carries the door it came through, and the Sunday digest counts them, so a door that brings nobody gets redesigned, not marketed.
20. **The club watches.** A club's week is a template it fills once: socials, americanos, level nights. Every slot becomes a public match on the club's page a few days ahead, organised by the person who claimed the club; players sign up and pull out themselves, from the page or the card in their chat. The club never approves a sign-up and never types a roster; staff open the day view and see how the day will run. A request for the club to confirm each player, for a checkout, or for a notification per sign-up is declined with this rule.
21. **A level is a claim until someone who saw you play confirms it.** The organizer of a finished match, a coach at the club, or the club confirms a level in one tap; the tick holds while results keep the number within half a step. An event that takes confirmed levels only treats a declared level inside its range like an out-of-range one: it goes to the organizer's list. From there the player asks a coach at the club, or the club, in one tap; the confirmation seats them on every such list that now lets them in, without the organizer typing: their level fits the range, or the range does not hold them (below). A player who already sat in a finished, scored match with the organiser skips that organiser's level-range approval. A session added this on 11 October 2026, without a decision. The owner then decided the same day, after an audit: the question was "Three identity and level limits had no decision from you. Which of these changes do you approve?", and the owner chose "Keep the past-partner waiver". The reason a session gave: the organiser saw them play, so that organiser's range (below it, above it) lets them straight in. Only the range: a confirmed-levels-only match still takes a confirmed level, a missing level is still asked for, and another organiser's range still holds them (`waivedByPlay` and `admissionAt`). "Finished, scored" means both held a taken seat, taken at or before the start, in a match that started, was not called off and has a score, a match's sets or a scored round of an americano (`playedOut`): the score typed in at the court counts at once, because the "Are you in?" after "Play again" comes before the booked length ends. A seat taken after the start never counts, because joining stays open until the booked length ends and any seated player may type the score; and once a match has started only its organiser moves its start, so a latecomer cannot move the start past their own seat. A request for a test, a video review, a paid verification or a hierarchy of badges is declined with this rule.
22. **An Open repeats by itself.** The organizer of a finished tournament turns it into a series in one screen: a name and a rhythm (same weekday and time, every week, fortnight or month). The next edition exists before the page reloads; the rest make themselves a few days ahead, list themselves on the series page and the city page, and close themselves like any tournament. The organizer never types a date or a roster again, and can pause. A request for per-edition approval, a registration desk, entry fees through us, or a bracket is declined with this rule; five series per organizer.
23. **A pair is the unit of the serious tournament.** A competition has categories; a pair enters a category, once, and a player enters at most two categories in the same competition. A full category takes the pair on its waiting list in order of entry, and a withdrawal moves the first waiting pair up and tells them. A declared level outside a category's band cannot enter from the page; the organiser's desk can enter anyone. A request for a solo entry, a third category, or a queue that jumps is declined with this rule.
24. **A partner is a name until they claim the spot.** Whoever enters names their partner; the partner confirms by link, signed in or by typing their name, and the placeholder folds into their account. Nothing beyond a name is asked for the partner, and nothing is sent to a placeholder. A request to require the partner's account, phone or email at entry is declined with this rule.
25. **The fee is text and "paid" is the organiser's mark.** The competition shows the amount per pair and the ways to pay as the organiser wrote them; the organiser marks a pair paid. No payment passes through Kicksmash (rule 13 for coaches says the same). A request for a checkout, a deposit, or a refund flow is declined with this rule.
26. **We link to the stream, we never host it.** A big match gets a "Watch live" link the organiser pastes (YouTube, Twitch, Facebook Live); the page and the TV screen show it. A request for video hosting, a player, or a broadcast overlay is declined with this rule.
27. **The draw is made whole, published once, and a result is final once built on.** The organiser makes the draw from the field as it is (seeds and wildcards as they set them), looks at it, redraws or publishes; the players hear once where they start. Every phase is generated at once and fills itself in as results land. A score comes from the organiser or from a player of either pair and is checked against the rule of its round; it can be corrected until the next match built on it has a score. A request for a hand-edited bracket, a re-seed after publication, or a result entered by a spectator is declined with this rule.
28. **The schedule is one button, and the organiser moves what they must.** Every match of every drawn category gets a court and a time from the court list and the day's window: the groups first, a rest between a pair's matches, a round after the round it is built on, the finals last, never one player on two courts. The organiser moves one match with a court and a time; both pairs hear. Every player hears their own list once, and the court fifteen minutes before each match. A request to schedule by hand from a blank grid, or to remind more than once, is declined with this rule.
29. **A result is one reply, and the screen tells the truth every thirty seconds.** A player answers the match notice in the chat with the score and it lands on that match; the desk types it on the page; either way the tables, the brackets, the club's screen and the podium follow without a tap. The podium is a moment for every player on it, once (rule 18). A request for a live-score widget per point, a spectator's score, or a leaderboard of who enters results is declined with this rule.
30. **A group shows names, not levels, and may ask to join.** The owner's decision E, 9 October 2026: "What does a visitor see on a group page, and who can join?" — "names visible, levels hidden, optional Ask to join", for social proof and privacy. On the group page's member list, in `GET /api/v1/groups/{code}` and in the MCP tool `get_group`, a visitor sees a crew's first names and how many they are; a member's level there is for the group's own members. A match's own roster is not part of this: it still shows the levels of its seated players to anybody, by the match page's own rule, and every match on the group page links to its roster. The group's own range stays visible, because it describes the crew. An admin may switch on Ask to join (a ladies' crew, a level crew): a newcomer asks, with an optional short note; an admin says yes or no; a declined person may ask again after seven days. A seat in such a group's match is only a seat. Nor is a name a way in. The owner decided on 10 October 2026: "Need a shared match first". So "Are these yours?" folds a same-name record that is a member of such a group only after a shared match, and the automatic fold at the proof of an address does the same. The owner extended this on 11 October 2026. The question was: "Three identity and level limits had no decision from you. Which of these changes do you approve?" The owner chose "No admin role by name alone": "In a crew without "Ask to join", "Are these yours?" can move a crew's admin role by name alone. The change refuses that, as "That's me" already does." So a same-name record that created a crew, or is an admin of a crew, also folds only after a shared match. This is true for every crew, also for a crew that does not ask to join. Both doors ask it: the claim on the card and the automatic fold at the proof. A shared match is a match that is not called off, where each record held a seat inside its capacity or was its organiser. A shared organiser, a shared club or a place on the waiting list is not a shared match (`mayClaim` in `src/lib/domain/sameName.ts`). A match still to come counts too, so a stranger of the same name can take a free seat beside the record and pass. The owner has not decided that case. For such a record, the card shows the days of its matches, but not the club and not the names of the other players, so that a stranger of the same name does not learn where to play to pass the check. One line takes the place of "Yes, these are mine": for a record that runs a crew, it says that the matches belong to somebody who runs a crew. A request to show members' levels to visitors on those three, to show who asked or what they wrote to anyone but the group's admins, or to make a match seat a way into an asking crew is declined with this rule.

## The crew's own chat

31. **The bot reads only where a crew asked it to, and keeps none of the words.** The owner, 9 October 2026: "players join a group managed by the Kicksmash bot so that it can assist setting up matches and do the admin, so there are no privacy concerns. The group with the bot that listens is solely for the purpose of coordinating padel matches." So the bot reads plain messages in one kind of place only: a Telegram group that an admin of that group tied to a crew through the link the crew page shows the crew's admins ("Run a Telegram group for this crew"), where the bot is an admin and its notice is pinned. The crew page asks for a new group only for the crew's padel. The link is signed for that crew admin and stops working when they stop being one. The first group that a crew ties stays the crew's group. The bot ties no second group to that crew, and another crew's link does not move the group. A session wrote this limit on 11 October 2026; the owner has not decided it. /quiet in the first group, or the bot's removal, frees the crew for another group. A first group that the bot is no longer in frees the crew too. The bot asks Telegram about that group once, when somebody taps the crew link in a new group. The bot stops reading when it loses the right to pin. The Bot API sends no update when somebody unpins a message. So an unpinned notice does not stop the reading, and a group admin types /quiet for that. Telegram's privacy mode stays on, so everywhere else the bot sees what it always saw: commands, and replies to its own messages (a reply "+1" or "can't make it" to a card takes or frees the seat in any group). In a crew's group it acts on four things and ignores the rest. A session wrote these fixed forms on 10 October 2026; the owner has not decided them. On 11 October 2026 a session added the common form with the question at the end (option C of the audit). Whether a model reads each line (option B) is the owner's decision. The first two things are "in" and "out" in English, Russian and Spanish, for the one open match. With two open matches, the bot asks that person alone, or says nothing. "me", "+" and their like count only as a reply to a card. A question, a forward or a channel's post never takes a seat. The third thing is "who's in Thursday 7pm Rawai?". It makes the match and posts its card as the reply, but only when the question names its hour outright. Its place must be a court the chat knows: its usual court, a court the sender played at, or a listed club named in full. Or it names no place, and the crew has a usual court. The question can also come after the match: "Let's play Thursday 7pm at Warehaus, who's in?". The words "padel" or "a game" name no place. A question with "no" and no court in it makes no match ("No padel tomorrow 7pm, anyone?"). A listed club named in full is its whole name, the name without "club" or "indoor", or every word that tells it apart. A city, a district or an everyday word such as "Asia" or "drama" never names a club. The owner, 10 October 2026: "Yes, but only on the club's own words". That match takes the day, the hour and the court, and nothing else from the chat line. A court the chat does not know yet goes through /new. A /new line in a crew's group puts the match on no club's board unless the line says "public". A stranger who joins from a board becomes a member of the crew, and the crew page gives members the group's invite link. A fourth thing came on 10 October 2026 (rule 36): "times?" or "when can we play?", the whole message and asked with its question mark (in a group "free courts" or "hay pistas" says something to the others and asks the bot nothing), gets one reply with up to three free courts as buttons, at most one a minute; a tap on one makes that match the same way, and when nothing is free the answer is 🤷. It keeps first names, Telegram usernames, who is in or out, the court and the score; it never keeps the text of a message, never answers chatter, never changes the group's name, photo or description, and answers a word with a reaction, not a message (rule 5). One exception: after a seat taken by a word or a tap, that person alone gets one ephemeral button, once. The button opens the calendar feed of their matches. A session added this on 11 October 2026 (option A of the audit); the owner has not decided it. The group sees nothing, and the feed signs nobody in. A player whose email address brings an invitation for each match gets no button. /quiet from a group admin (an anonymous one too) stops it acting on words at once. The crew page then stops showing the group's invite link. The bot stays an admin, so Telegram still sends it the group's messages. It does not act on them, and only taking its admin rights away, or removing it, stops them. The invite link also goes when the bot loses the right to invite. A request to read a group that did not opt in, to keep what people wrote, to summarise a chat, or to make the bot talk in a crew's group is declined with this rule. The same decision of 9 October 2026 started the clock for WhatsApp: "Telegram crew group now, Meta clock starts today". Meta's Groups API lets a business make its own group, and players join it by an invite link. It needs an Official Business Account, allows 8 people at most, and allows no edit of a message (Meta's page, read 11 October 2026). So a WhatsApp crew group is a group that the bot makes, with a new card at each change, as on LINE. It waits for the owner's Meta steps (`ROADMAP.md`, "Next, in order", item 6).

## Social nights

32. **A fixed pair plays the night together.** The owner's decision F, 9 October 2026: "Fixed pairs in social tournaments (two partners play every round together), as Phuket nights often do." Agreed with it: pairs sign up together, with a "Partner needed" row for a single player, and pair results still move levels. A social tournament (americano, mexicano or King of the Court) may keep fixed pairs instead of rotating partners; it is off by default. A partner is a name until they claim the spot (rule 24), and a player alone is listed as "Partner needed" until somebody taps "Be their partner" or the organiser pairs two singles. The pair is the unit of the draw, the rests and the table: americano rotates the opponents so every pair meets every other pair, nobody's pair rests twice before every pair has rested once, round 1 takes two complete pairs or more, and a player without a partner is not drawn. A pair's place in the night's table moves each partner's level once, as a player's place does on a night of rotating partners; both partners move by the same step, and a partner without a level does not move. The night stays light: a request for a category, an entry fee, a seeded draw or a bracket on a social night is declined with this rule, and the serious tournament (rules 23 to 29) is where those live.

## Notices

33. **A switch for each kind, quiet hours, and an inbox.** The owner's decision D, 9 October 2026: "A switch for each notice kind, quiet hours, and an inbox." Seven kinds, in a player's words: match changes, reminders, open spots, club matches, scores and results, the crew, lessons. All are on until the player says otherwise, but club matches, which the owner's decision B of the same day made an opt-in ("the club-match email becomes opt-in"), so it is off by default for every channel. Quiet hours are the player's own night, read in the zone their browser named; inside them nothing is delivered, unless the notice is about a match that starts within three hours. Every notice the app sends a player lands first in their inbox on My matches and stays there ninety days, so none is lost: a kind switched off is kept there and never sent, not even later, and what quiet hours held goes out as one short message per channel when they end ("3 updates while you were away"), never one by one. The old switch for activity emails keeps exactly its old meaning beside the kinds: it is a switch on the email channel, not on a kind. The answer to something the person asked for themselves (the calendar invitation of a match they just joined, a code, their link, a claim decided, the thank-you for a note) is not a notice, and no switch holds it. A request for a notice that skips the inbox, a kind on by default that a player did not ask for, a second summary, or a notice switched off by somebody other than the player is declined with this rule.

## A name as a way back in

34. **"That's me" signs in a record that has nothing to prove it.** The owner, 10 October 2026: "Yes, 'That's me' signs in." A link in a WhatsApp group opens the phone's default browser, and a browser that does not know the player asks for a name and makes a new record; the ways back with proof (an email code, Telegram) reached 2 of 21 players. ## Free court times

35. **We read the free court times that clubs share or that platforms show publicly, and the player books and pays.** The owner, 10 October 2026: "platforms forbid scraping in their terms but we are just testing, and on top every app scrapes every other app, for example padel society scrapes playtomic and matchi for court availability. Earlier the same day: "we could also build a scraper to every 15min check all courts and times available on playtomic, padel society, matchi and other apps used in Thailand and Singapore, and then propose the best times for players". So Kicksmash reads the free court times of the clubs it lists. A feed the club shares is read every hour and always wins. Where there is none, Kicksmash reads what an anonymous visitor's browser loads on the booking platform's public club page: today's times every 15 minutes for every club that a reader can read, and the next two days about once an hour. (Until 11 October 2026, only a club that people used was read every 15 minutes. Production had no such club on a platform, so each club was read once an hour.) We accept the risk that a platform blocks us. So robots.txt does not bind a reader under this rule: the Playtomic reader reads paths that Playtomic's robots.txt disallows, and the MATCHi reader keeps out of the paths MATCHi's names only as a courtesy. We read slowly and say who we are: the name KicksmashBot, a page that says what it reads and where to write (/about), one request a second for each platform, and a cache of every page. A 401, 403 or 429, or a challenge (a WAF header, a 202 or a 405), stops that platform and it rests for six hours, then a day, then a week. Free court times read from a platform are facts with their source named, not ours to license: they are not under CC BY 4.0 (rule 6), and every page and the API name the platform. Before this rule, a line in `src/lib/booking/availability.ts` said "No scraping, no credentials, nothing a club did not hand us". A session wrote it on 7 September 2026, and the owner never asked for it. **A pasted link is the one exception to the name.** The owner, 11 October 2026: "“we do not pretend to be WhatsApp's link-preview robot to get a preview” why would you think that? that’s a great idea to get the match details, do it, update the rules if that is conflict somewhere". When a person pastes a booking link, Kicksmash reads that link once, as the link-preview robot of a chat app reads it. The platform gives that robot the preview that it gives the person's own chat. Kicksmash makes one read for each paste: one request, and at most three redirects on the platform's own hosts. It sends it only to a host from the list of platforms (`PLATFORMS`), and a host goes on that list only when the platform holds it. The bot's "Create match" tap does not read the link again. Kicksmash does not read a platform that is switched off or that rests after a block, as the reader of free court times does not. It keeps nothing but the match that the person makes from the paste. Its User-Agent names the chat app's robot first, then KicksmashBot and /about (`src/lib/booking/unfurl.ts`). The reader of free court times keeps the name KicksmashBot alone. **A sign-in to read the free times.** The owner, 10 October 2026: "if a sign-in is required to read available times, I will provide my own sign-in for testing purposes. Let me know if needed and I'll provide." The Padel Society shows free courts only in its app, and nobody has checked yet whether that app needs a sign-in for them. The owner has not yet decided whether Kicksmash may use the offer. Today Kicksmash never signs in to any platform, with a player's account or with an account of its own. A session wrote "or its own" on 10 October 2026; the owner has not decided it. Today Kicksmash also never calls a platform's private API, an address that only the platform's own app calls. A session wrote that limit on 11 October 2026; the owner has not decided it. The Playtomic reader calls the JSON address that Playtomic's own public club page calls (`/api/clubs/availability`), with no sign-in. Kicksmash never stores a player's password, token or session. It never books, reserves, pays or posts anything on a platform. It never gets around a login wall, a captcha or a block. It does not change its address and does not solve a challenge. It poses as a chat app's robot only for the one read of a pasted link. That read stops at the first 401, 403 or 429. Nothing tries it again. The player books and pays on the platform. This rule declines a request to book or pay for a player, or to sign in as a player (the owner's choice of 10 October 2026, "Best times + one-tap booking by a player"). ## Courts

36. **Kicksmash organises the court; a player books it and pays.** The owner's choice of 10 October 2026, "Best times + one-tap booking by a player", and the same day: "kicksmash can do everything except the actual payment; this means create the booking and then hand off to the human to complete the payment." Kicksmash reads the free times that clubs share or that platforms show publicly (the owner's decision of 10 October 2026, accepting the risk of being blocked; rule 35). It does less than the owner's words: it creates no booking. "Book this court" opens a page, and the player picks the court and the time, signs in, books and pays there. Kicksmash never signs in as a player and never pays. A booking that waits for the player's payment needs access from a platform or a club. Only the owner can ask for that access. The free times are read by the jobs of rule 35 and cached on the club's row, never fetched while a person waits, and ranked a usual club at a usual time first, then the soonest (`src/lib/domain/bestTimes.ts`), with the club's own feed winning over a platform's read and a platform's times named as the platform's. They appear where people choose a time: in the create form's row of time chips, beside the person's usual times, on /play below the games while a city has few open games, and in a crew's chat or a private chat only when somebody asks ("times?", /times), as one reply (rule 5). "Book this court" opens the club's own booking page, on the match's day where a platform's public page takes the day (`src/lib/booking/prepare.ts`), with the court, day, time and length beside it; the player's browser opens it, it carries nobody's identity, and a chat message that carries it shows no link preview. "Book and pay", a checkout already filled in, waits for the owner's decision. The Playtomic reader can build Playtomic's own checkout link for each free court, and nothing hands that link to a player. A session wrote on 10 October 2026 that the checkout never uses a platform's internal route; the owner has not decided that limit. "I booked it" is a player's mark, who and when and nothing more, which any player of the match can take back (rules 13 and 25 say the same of "paid"). A court that a player already booked comes in the other way. The player pastes the booking's link or its share text on the create form or to the Telegram bot, or forwards it to book@. Once the owner's WhatsApp number is set, the WhatsApp thread takes the same paste. Kicksmash then fills the match form with what the paste gives. A day or a time that the paste does not give keeps the form's own, and the form says that it is not the booking's. The zone is the club's, else the zone of the browser or of the chat. When no zone is known, the form uses the person's own zone, never UTC. The player checks the form and creates the match. Rule 35 says how Kicksmash reads that link. No password, session or payment detail of anybody's platform account is stored, ever. A request to sign in to a platform as a player, to store a platform login, to pay or to hold a payment, or to book in a player's name without that player is declined with this rule.

   **A booking by email.** The owner decided on 11 October 2026. The question was: "Which email address reads a forwarded booking confirmation and answers with the filled match form?" ## The next game

37. **"Play again" makes the next game, and the players of the last one hear once.** The owner's decision of 11 October 2026: build option A, "Make the one working group repeat itself", and to "After an organiser taps 'Play again', can Kicksmash send one 'I'm in' message to each player of the last match?" the answer "Yes, one message each". "Play again" sits on the score panel once a result exists and asks for one thing, the day and the time, in one control above the button; the default is the last game's day and time a week on, counted on the wall clock, so one tap still makes next week's game. Everything else carries over: the club, the court, the length, the format, the names of a night's courts, the levels, the price, the capacity, the tags and fixed pairs, and a crew's match stays the crew's. A platform's invitation to one booking does not carry over, because it belongs to that booking. Each player who held a taken seat in the last match, but the person who tapped, gets one message, "Ana made the next game: Sat, Oct 17 18:00 at Warehaus. Are you in?", with the name of the next game's organiser. It is a notice of the crew kind (rule 33), so the player's switch and quiet hours hold it and the inbox keeps it. In Telegram it carries the card's own one-tap join; every link in it is the public match page, never a personal link, because a forwarded message keeps its buttons, and its email leaves out the "get this on Telegram" line, whose link binds whoever taps it. Outside Telegram, the email and the push have one line under the question, and that line names no button. The email has an "I'm in" button. The push has no button, and a tap on the push opens the same link. The link opens the public match page with the join step open (`?join=1`), and the player taps once more there. For a player whose browser knows them and who needs no level, that step is the page's own Join button. The link itself joins nobody, so a mail scanner that opens it changes nothing. A join in one tap with no browser session needs a personal link, and the forwarding rule above forbids that. One round per old match, however often anybody taps, and only for a match that was played (started, not called off, with a score): a tap on a match nobody has played yet sends nothing and leaves the round for after the game. Only the organiser, or a player who held a seat in its line-up from before the start, sends the round; a tap by a player who joined after the start sends nothing. A second tap at the day and time the round announced lands on that game rather than making a second one, and two taps at once, or a tap beside the hourly job, make one game at a crew's hour. The next game can exist already: the game the round announced, or a game that the same organiser made at the same club in the next 14 days, by hand or not. That game belongs to the same crew as the last match, or to no crew when the last match has none, so one crew's tap never lands on another crew's game. Then the score panel names it ("Next game", with its link), and the control shows its day and time. A tap at that day and time lands on that game and makes no second game, so the week is not split in two. The waiting list, a seat held for a name and a deleted account hear nothing, and no WhatsApp template carries it yet, so a player whose only channel is WhatsApp finds it in the inbox. "Make it weekly" sits under it, the quieter button, and does something else: it makes the crew a group with a weekly slot, so each week's card posts itself. The two never make two games at one hour, and nobody hears about one game twice. When the crew already has a game at the hour picked (the weekly slot made it), "Play again" lands on that game, and the round announces it to the players of the last match who have no place in it and no crew notice of it. When "Play again" made the game first, the game becomes the crew's, the slot makes no game at that hour, and the crew members who have no place in it and no notice of it get the crew notice once. A request for a second round of messages, a message to people who were not in the last match, or a reminder to those who did not answer is declined with this rule and rule 5.

   **Decision B: a second organiser in Phuket.** The owner chose "A now + B this week (Recommended)" on 11 October 2026. B was: "You ask one other Phuket organiser to run a weekly game for 4 weeks." ## Backups

38. **The backups keep about sixty days, and the history goes with them.** The owner decided on 10 October 2026. The question was: "How do we keep the nightly backups, so that the privacy page can promise a limit?" The answer was "Rebuild the history each night (Recommended)". Each night, the hourly job writes a copy of every table to the owner's private GitHub repository (`runBackup` in `src/lib/backup.ts`). Then it deletes the night files older than `BACKUP_KEEP_DAYS` (60) days. It keeps the cutoff day, so the folder holds 61 nights. Then it rebuilds the history of the repository with only those files. GitHub deletes the older commits later, at a time that it does not give. Until then, a person with access to the repository can read an old commit by its id. So /privacy says "about the last 60 days" and says that GitHub deletes the older copies later. The page takes the figure from the code (`src/lib/legal.ts`), and `tests/legal.test.ts` holds the two together. The service board turns yellow when the last rebuild is more than two days older than the last backup (docs/OPERATING.md). A request to keep the backups longer changes what /privacy promises about personal data, so it is the owner's decision (rule 9).
